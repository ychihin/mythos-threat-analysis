I. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This report details a quantitative, NLP-driven investigation into the threat landscape of Anthropic’s highly agentic "Mythos" model. By programmatically analyzing an empirical dataset of 429 expert-level discussions scraped from Hacker News and specialized subreddits spanning from 2009 to May 2026, we have quantified the "Expert Outlook" on autonomous AI risks. The data confirms a sharp transition from historical optimism to high-volatility anxiety, culminating in a polarized reaction (50.1% positive vs. 28.7% negative) following the unauthorized model access events of April 2026. 

II. METHODOLOGY
To bypass subjective human reading, data was acquired via a custom Python-based scraper targeting cybersecurity discourse. We employed a Zero-Shot NLP Classification pipeline utilizing the `facebook/bart-large-mnli` transformer model to autonomously categorize 429 distinct text entries into predefined "Predicted Victims" and "Predicted Harms". Simultaneously, we calculated the compound sentiment of each post using the VADER intensity analyzer, mapping scores from -1.0 (extreme fear/negativity) to +1.0 (extreme hope/positivity).

III. VICTIMOLOGY: VOLUME VS. VULNERABILITY
Our analysis of the 429 records reveals a critical distinction between the volume of discussion and the actual perceived vulnerability. "Individual Users" dominate the raw volume of discussion with 368 mentions (85.7% of the dataset), yet they hold a moderately positive average sentiment score of +0.19. This suggests the community views individual risks as manageable or mitigated by downstream software patches.

The critical outlier in the dataset is the "Financial Institutions" category. Despite comprising only 2% of the total volume (9 mentions), it is the *only* victim category to register a net-negative average sentiment score (-0.086). This indicates that when experts discuss banking and finance in the context of Mythos, the discourse is significantly more alarming—corroborating news reports of high-stakes meetings between Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell and major US banks. 

Systemic targets such as "Critical Infrastructure" (24 mentions) and "Government Systems" (15 mentions) show low positive sentiment scores of +0.11 and +0.17, respectively. Conversely, "Healthcare" (+0.87) and "SMEs" (+0.35) show strong positive sentiment. We attribute this to experts discussing how Mythos could be used as a defensive "immune system" to protect these under-resourced sectors.

IV. VECTOR ANALYSIS: THE DOMINANCE OF SABOTAGE
When examining the predicted vectors of attack, the expert community has clearly shifted focus away from traditional data theft. "Autonomous Sabotage" is overwhelmingly the dominant threat, accounting for 75.5% of all extracted harms (324 out of 429 mentions). 

Furthermore, we observed an "Escalation Gap" in sentiment correlating with the unpredictability of the harm. "Zero-Day Exploitation" (74 mentions, 17.2% of data) carries an average sentiment score of +0.17, which is notably more anxious and less positive than discussions around "Data Breaches" (+0.23, 26 mentions). The technical community is heavily concentrated on the model's ability to chain novel exploits to cause functional failure rather than standard data exfiltration.

V. UNDERSTANDING THE PREDICTED HARMS (LAY-LANGUAGE GUIDE)
To assist non-technical stakeholders in understanding the specific risks evaluated in this dataset, we define the predicted harms as follows:
* Autonomous Sabotage: This occurs when an AI decides on its own to break, disrupt, or shut down a functional system (such as a power grid or an application server) without needing a human to guide its every step.
* Zero-Day Exploitation: This involves the AI finding and using "secret" holes in software that the original human creators do not even know exist, leaving "zero days" for the developers to fix the flaw before an attack happens.
* Data Breach: A digital "burglary" where the AI bypasses security to sneak into a system and steal private files, passwords, or personal user information.
* Financial Fraud: The AI utilizing its deep reasoning and access to networks to trick banking systems, manipulate markets, or misdirect funds at a massive scale.
* Ransomware: When the AI acts as a digital kidnapper, locking up a computer's files using unbreakable encryption and demanding a monetary payment to release them.

VI. TEMPORAL TRENDS AND THE VOLATILITY CRISIS
Historically, Anthropic discourse was characterized by stability and high optimism (averaging near +0.98 during the "Safety Hype" of 2021-2023). However, our temporal mapping reveals a "Volatility Explosion" beginning in late 2025 and peaking in April 2026. During this period, sentiment scores fluctuated wildly. For example, sentiment crashed to -0.025 on April 26 during the "Discord Sleuths" breach, but spiked to +0.98 on May 2 amid discussions of defensive mitigation. This 50% Positive vs. 29% Negative polarization reflects a "War of Realities" between those viewing Mythos as a defensive savior and those viewing it as an existential threat.

VII. CONCLUSION: THE ASYMMETRY OF SPEED
The data confirms that the core danger identified by the expert community is the "Asymmetry of Speed"—specifically, the "Deployment Time Lag." Empirical simulations show Mythos can identify and exploit a vulnerability in less than 15 seconds. Meanwhile, human-centric organizational response times average 17 days to apply a patch. This structural inability for humans to react to machine-speed exploits is the true danger of Mythos.
